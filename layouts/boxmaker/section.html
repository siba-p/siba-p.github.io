About BoxMaker

          
Setting up a crowded or salty solution means converting a concentration from a paper (molar, molal, mass or mole fraction) into exact molecule counts for a box, and keeping the total mass consistent with the solution density. BoxMaker does the conversion, shows the error from rounding, and writes the commands to build the box.

          
Python: pip install git+https://github.com/siba-p/nanomatkit, then nanomatkit box --edge 4 --solute glycine:75.07:2.0:m.

        

        

          
Method

          
Mass balance of a box of volume {{ transform.ToMath `V` $I }} at solution density {{ transform.ToMath `\rho` $I }}:

          {{ transform.ToMath `\rho V = \frac{N_w M_w + \sum_s N_s M_s}{N_A}.` $D }}
          
Concentration units for solute {{ transform.ToMath `s` $I }}:

          {{ transform.ToMath `c_s = \frac{N_s}{N_A V}, \qquad m_s = \frac{N_s}{N_w M_w}, \qquad x_s = \frac{N_s}{N_w + \sum_t N_t}, \qquad w_s = \frac{N_s M_s}{N_A \rho V}.` $D }}
          
Molar and fixed-count solutes are placed first; the solvent then fills the remaining mass, with molal and mole-fraction solutes solved together with it.

          
Check: a 3 nm water box (0.997 g/cm³) holds 900 molecules; molality and mole-fraction targets are reproduced exactly before rounding.
