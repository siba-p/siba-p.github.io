I use statistical mechanics, molecular simulation and machine learning to understand functional nanomaterials, and then to design them.

My doctoral work at IIT Delhi, with Prof. Divya Nayar, asked how gold nanoparticles coated with DNA or citrate behave in crowded, chemically complex surroundings. All-atom simulations and Kirkwood–Buff solvation theory showed that molecular chemistry, not excluded volume alone, sets their size, stability and binding. The thesis closed by turning hundreds of thousands of free-energy calculations into attention-based neural networks that design surfaces for a chosen polymer adhesion.

Today I continue this work at QpiAI, building simulation and AI methods for materials discovery.

Crowding & solvation
