From g(r) to thermodynamics

Kirkwood–Buff integrals turn radial distribution functions into preferential binding, activity coefficients and partial molar volumes, but only if the finite size of the box is handled properly. Load your RDFs, or explore an example where the right answer is known:

Why KBkit?

The textbook running integral G(R) = 4π∫₀ᴿ[g(r) − 1]r²dr does not converge in a closed, finite simulation box. In such a box, even an ideal gas looks as if its particles avoid each other. KBkit computes the raw integral alongside two standard corrections, shows whether they agree, and only then turns the result into thermodynamics.

Python package and command line: pip install git+https://github.com/siba-p/kbkit. The web app and the package give identical numbers.

Method

  • GvdV: g(r) corrected for the depletion of a finite box, then integrated.
  • KV: finite-volume integral with weight 1 − 3x/2 + x³/2, extrapolated through L·GV(L) = G∞L + F.
  • Reliability score: agreement between the two corrected estimates, flatness of the plateau, and whether the correction could be applied.
  • Thermodynamics: Δ = Gcc + Gww − 2Gcw; ∂ln ac/∂ln xc = 1/(1 + ρxwΔ); V̄ and κT from η and ζ; νpc = ρc(Gpc − Gpw).
