Load your RDFs, or try the demo: a hard-sphere fluid at packing fraction 0.30, whose Kirkwood–Buff integral is known exactly from the Carnahan–Starling equation of state. You will see how the closed-box bias in the raw integral is removed by the Ganguly–van der Vegt and Krüger–Vlugt corrections.

Why KBkit?

Kirkwood–Buff integrals connect RDFs to thermodynamics: preferential binding, activity derivatives, partial molar volumes and compressibility. The textbook running integral does not converge in a closed, finite simulation box, and even a pure ideal gas appears attractive. KBkit computes the raw integral alongside two standard corrections, so you can see at a glance whether your number can be trusted.

Method

  • Raw: G(R) = 4π∫₀ᴿ[g(r) − 1]r²dr.
  • GvdV: g(r) corrected for the depletion of a finite box (Ganguly & van der Vegt, JCTC 2013).
  • KV: finite-volume integral with weight 1 − 3x/2 + x³/2, extrapolated as L·GV(L) = G∞L + F (Krüger, Vlugt et al., JPCL 2013).
  • Thermodynamics: standard KB relations for binary mixtures and preferential binding.

Validated against hard spheres and the ideal gas (KV within 1% of the exact result). Python package and CLI: github.com/siba-p/kbkit.
