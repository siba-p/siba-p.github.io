Issues

          
    
        
    
      
    
      
    
        
    Active learning · Gaussian-process umbrella integration AI/ML
    
        
    Each window's spring balance gives a noisy measurement of the mean force, k(c − ⟨ξ⟩). A Gaussian process turns these into a PMF with a Bayesian 95% band and finds where one more window would reduce the uncertainty most.
    
        
    
        
    
      
    
      
    
        
    Suggested repair windows
    
        
    Centres are offset by F′/k so that each new window samples the middle of the gap; force constants are raised where the PMF is steep.
    
        
    
      
    
    
    
  
    

  
    
    
    
      
    
        
    Why WindowWise?
    
        
    Umbrella sampling fails quietly. Windows that are too far apart, or springs too soft for a steep wall, leave holes in the free-energy profile that WHAM bridges anyway. You usually find out only after the simulations have finished.
    
        
    WindowWise brings the design and the post-mortem into one place. It plans spacing and force constants from first principles before you simulate, then runs WHAM with bootstrap errors on your pullx files and points to the exact windows to add or stiffen.
    
      
    
      
    
        
    Method
    
        
      
          
    • Planning. Neighbouring windows of width σ = √(kBT/k) separated by d overlap by 2Φ(−d/2σ). The force constant is chosen so that the steepest slope shifts a window by at most half a spacing.
      • 
          
    • WHAM. Self-consistent 1D WHAM (Kumar et al., 1992) on a common histogram grid. Bias U = ½k(ξ − ξ₀)², as in the GROMACS pull code.
      • 
          
    • Errors. Block bootstrap of each time series, with block length set by the statistical inefficiency.
      • 
          
    • Repair. Gaps below the overlap threshold get new windows. Their spacing follows the target overlap, their k is raised where the local slope is steep, and their centres are offset by F′/k.
      • 
          
    • GP umbrella integration (ML). Window mean forces k(c − ⟨ξ⟩), with errors from the statistical inefficiency, are derivative observations of a Gaussian-process prior on the PMF (cf. Stecher, Bernstein & Csányi, JCTC 2014). Active learning picks new windows where the posterior mean-force uncertainty is largest.
      • 
        
    
        
    Validated against an analytic Morse-type PMF: WHAM RMSE 0.19 kJ/mol over a 30 kJ/mol well; GP RMSE 0.18–0.86 kJ/mol with 41 to 11 windows. Python package and CLI: github.com/siba-p/windowwise.
