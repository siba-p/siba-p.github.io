4.2 Deriving the Boltzmann factor

A system in contact with a heat bath at temperature T is found in a state of energy En with a probability that falls exponentially with energy. Ludwig Boltzmann reached this result in 1877 by counting the number of ways the energy of a gas can be shared among its molecules.

The Boltzmann distribution

{{ transform.ToMath "p_n = \\frac{e^{-\\textcolor{#e8792b}{E_n}/k_B\\textcolor{#12a594}{T}}}{Z}, \\qquad Z = \\sum_m e^{-E_m/k_BT}" (dict "displayMode" true) }}
  • {{ transform.ToMath "\\textcolor{#e8792b}{E_n}" }}: energy of state n
  • {{ transform.ToMath "\\textcolor{#12a594}{T}" }}: temperature of the bath
  • {{ transform.ToMath "Z" }}: the partition function, which normalises

High-energy states are exponentially rare, and temperature sets how rare.

5 Key pointsSelf-check questionsReferences
  1. One page per chapter. Each book opens with a syllabus: its parts, what every chapter teaches and what it builds on. Inside a chapter the sidebar shows the whole book and follows the section you are reading; on a phone it opens from the button at the top.
  2. A clear start. Every chapter opens with why the idea matters, a short list of what you will be able to do, and the chapters to read first. Then the text begins with the question that motivated the idea and the people and papers that answered it.
  3. Equation cards. Key equations are set apart with every symbol explained and a one-line reading in words. Colours in the equation match the colours in the figures.
  4. Interactive figures and code. Figures compute their results as you change the parameters. Code cells run real Python with NumPy in the page; the first run downloads the interpreter, about 10 MB.
  5. A clear finish. Each chapter closes with its key points, a quiz that explains its answers, the original papers, and a link to the next chapter. Mark chapters as read to track your progress in this browser.
